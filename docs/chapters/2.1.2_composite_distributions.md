### Composite distributions 
This section contains composite distributions in the sense that they refer to other distribution which they combine or modify in some way. 

#### Composition of factors {#sec:composition}

Distributions that multiply other distributions do so in one of two ways:

- **Product composition**: the factors are multiplied as listed. The distribution is *opaque*: towards any distribution that contains it, it is a single factor.
- **Union composition**: the distribution stands for a *set* of factors, and its density is the product over this set. The set is obtained by replacing every component that itself uses union composition by the factors of that component, recursively. Identical factors (see [Identity](#sec:identity){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:identity"}) are contained in the set once, however many times and under whatever names they occur. The distribution is *transparent*.

Two factors of such a set that are not identical [must not]{.smallcaps} share a variate. An implementation that has not established the identity of two factors that share a variate [must]{.smallcaps} reject the model; it [must not]{.smallcaps} multiply both.

In this version of the standard, the [Combined distribution](#sec:combined-distribution){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:combined-distribution"} and the [Array distribution](#sec:array-distribution){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:array-distribution"} use union composition. All other distributions use product composition.

*Example (non-normative): Let $A$ and $B$ be combined distributions with the components $\{a, Z_a\}$ and $\{b, Z_b\}$, where $Z_a$ is an Array distribution over $x_1,\ldots,x_4$ and $Z_b$ one over $x_3,\ldots,x_6$, with identical components for $x_3$ and $x_4$. A union of $A$ and $B$ has the factors $a$, $b$ and one factor for each of $x_1,\ldots,x_6$. The components for $x_3$ and $x_4$ enter once.*


#### Mixture distribution {#sec:mixture-distribution label="sec:mixture-distribution"}

The mixture distribution, sometimes called addition of distributions, is a (weighted) sum of distributions $f_i(\vec{x})$, depending on the same variable(s) $\vec{x}$: 

$$
\begin{aligned} \text{MixturePdf}(\vec{x}) = \frac{1}{\ensuremath{\mathcal{M}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n} c_i \cdot f_i(\vec{x}) , \end{aligned} 
$$



where the $c_i$ are coefficients and $\vec{x}$ is the vector of variables. 

- `name`: custom unique string 
- `type`: `mixture_dist` 
- `x`: name of the variable $x$ ([optional]{.smallcaps}, since the     variable is fully defined by the summands) 
- `summands`: array of names referencing distributions 
- `coefficients`: array of names of coefficients $c_i$ or numbers to     be added 
- `extended`: boolean denoting whether this is an extended distribution ([required]{.smallcaps}, see below) 
This distribution can be treated as extended or as non-extended. 
- If the number of coefficients equals the number of distributions and `extended` is `false`, then the sum of the coefficient must be     (approximately) one. 
- If the number of coefficients equals the number of distributions and `extended` is `true`, then the sum of the coefficients will be used     as the Poisson rate parameter and the mixture distribution itself     will use the coefficients normalized by their sum. 
- If the number of coefficients is one less than the number of     distributions, then `extended` must be `false` and $c_n$ is computed     from the other coefficients as 



$$
\begin{aligned}     c_n &= 1 - \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}c_i .     \end{aligned} 
$$


The component `extended` is [required]{.smallcaps}. If the number of coefficients equals the number of distributions, it alone decides whether the distribution carries a Poisson term for the total number of events, and a document that omits it is invalid. If the number of coefficients is one less than the number of distributions, `extended` [must]{.smallcaps} be `false`; implementations [may]{.smallcaps}, at their discretion, accept documents that omit it in this case. A mixture distribution without coefficients, whose summands are all extended, is not defined in this version of the standard.


#### Mixture model

**Provisional.** This type is provisional. The representation of resolution models and analytical convolutions is under revision, and this type is expected to be replaced in a future version of this standard, possibly without a backward-compatibility provision. Documents intended for long-term preservation [should not]{.smallcaps} rely on it.

The mixture model is a weighted sum of resolution models or model PDFs:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{MixtureModel}(x) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} c_i\,m_i(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

If one coefficient fewer than the number of summands is provided, the final coefficient is computed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_n = 1 - \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} c_i .
\end{aligned}
$$

If all coefficients are given, their sum is used as the expected number of events in extended use cases.

-   `name`: custom unique string
-   `type`: `mixture_model`
-   `summands`: array of names referencing model distributions
-   `coefficients`: array of names of coefficients $c_i$ or numbers
-   `extended`: boolean denoting whether this is an extended model  


#### Combined distribution {#sec:combined-distribution label="sec:combined-distribution"}

The combined distribution is the product of its sub-distributions $m_c$ over the sub-data sets $x_c$ of the paired combined data set, selected by `key` $c$ as described in [Selection keys](#sec:joint-selection):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{CombinedPdf}(\{x_c\}) &= \prod_{c} \text{PDF}(m_c(\theta), x_c),
\end{aligned}
$$

where the product runs over the keys with a sub-data set. Each $m_c$ is evaluated on the sub-data set with its `key` exactly as if paired with it directly; an extended $m_c$ contributes its own Poisson term. The combined distribution has no expected event count and defines no distribution over the keys: a `combined_dist` [must not]{.smallcaps} have the component `extended`.

Sampling is defined per component: an extended component draws its own number of entries; for a non-extended component it [must]{.smallcaps} be supplied by the caller.

*Remark (non-normative): If every component is extended, with expected event counts $\nu_c$, this is, up to a parameter-independent factor, a single Poisson point process with rate $\nu = \sum_c \nu_c$ whose outcomes carry the `key` with probability $\nu_c/\nu$.*

- `name`: custom unique string
- `type`: `combined_dist`
- `columns`: array of names, [must]{.smallcaps} equal the `columns` of the paired combined data set, in the same order. Together with a `key` they select one sub-distribution, see [Selection keys](#sec:joint-selection).
- `components`: array of structs, each assigning one `key` to the distribution used for it:
    - `key`: array of strings, one per entry of `columns`, as described in [Selection keys](#sec:joint-selection). No two entries of `components` [may]{.smallcaps} have the same `key`.
    - `dist`: name of the sub-distribution used for this `key`

The combined distribution uses union composition (see [Composition of factors](#sec:composition){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:composition"}). Apart from identical factors, which enter once, the distributions referenced by `components` [must]{.smallcaps} be statistically independent of each other, and no variate [may]{.smallcaps} be used by more than one of them. If identical factors occur in components with different `key`s, the sub-data sets with these `key`s [must]{.smallcaps} supply the same observed values for the variates of these factors; otherwise the pairing is invalid. The sub-distribution with a given `key` is evaluated on the sub-data set with the same `key` in the paired combined data set; its variates are matched to the axes of that sub-data set by name, as for any other pairing of a distribution with a data set. Since the variates are, apart from those of identical factors, pairwise distinct across components, the pairing by `key` is a cross-check of the pairing by variate names and [must]{.smallcaps} agree with it. `components` [need not]{.smallcaps} list every possible `key`. The paired combined data set [must not]{.smallcaps} list a `key` that has no entry in `components`. A `key` of `components` without a sub-data set in the paired combined data set contributes no factor. A `key` whose sub-data set is present always contributes, also with zero events: for an extended sub-distribution with expected event count $\nu_c$, a present unbinned sub-data set with zero entries still contributes the factor $\exp(-\nu_c)$. A sub-data set that is present but has no entries contributes no factor for a non-extended component.

The `columns` and `key`s are labels. They select the sub-data set a component is paired with and have no mathematical meaning: the density depends only on the set of factors. A component [may]{.smallcaps} itself be a combined distribution, in which case the sub-data set with its `key` [must]{.smallcaps} be a combined data set that can be paired with it. When an implementation replaces such a component by its factors, it [may]{.smallcaps} relabel `columns` and `key`s in any way, as long as every factor that remains has a `key` that is unique within the resulting distribution. Of several identical factors, it [may]{.smallcaps} keep any one and drop the others together with their `key`s.

```json title="Example: Combined distribution for a multi-channel model with global observables"
{
    "name":"channels",
    "type":"combined_dist",
    "columns":["regions","observables"],
    "components":[
        {"key":["SR","physics"], "dist":"sr"},
        {"key":["CR","physics"], "dist":"cr"},
        {"key":["VR","physics"], "dist":"vr"},
        {"key":["SR","global"],  "dist":"global_sr"},
        {"key":["CR","global"],  "dist":"global_cr"}
    ]
}
```


#### Product distribution {#sec:product-distribution label="sec:product-distribution"} 

The product of independent distributions $f_i$ is defined as 



$$
\begin{aligned} \text{ProductPdf}(\vec{x}) &= \frac{1}{\ensuremath{\mathcal{M}}}\prod_{i=1}^n f_i(\vec{x}_i), \end{aligned} 
$$



where $\vec{x}_i$ denotes the variates of the factor $f_i$ and $\vec{x}$ the record of all of them.

-   `name`: custom string 
-   `type`: `product_dist` 
-   `factors`: array of names referencing distributions 

The product distribution uses product composition (see [Composition of factors](#sec:composition){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:composition"}): the product distribution as a whole is a single factor of any distribution that contains it. The behavior of a product distribution whose factors have overlapping variates, including one in which two entries of `factors` are identical, is unspecified in this version of the standard. Documents [should not]{.smallcaps} rely on it.

#### Efficiency product distribution

The efficiency product distribution multiplies a normalized PDF with an efficiency function and normalizes the product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{EfficiencyProductPdf}(x) =
\frac{1}{\ensuremath{\mathcal{M}}}\, f(x)\,\epsilon(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

The efficiency function is expected to be non-negative over the domain.

-   `name`: custom unique string
-   `type`: `efficiency_product_pdf_dist`
-   `pdf`: name of the input distribution $f$
-   `eff`: name of the efficiency function $\epsilon$

#### Density Function distribution 

A distribution that is specified via a non-normalized density function $f(x)$. Formally, it corresponds to the probability measure whose density in respect to the Lebesgue measure is proportional to $f(x)$. 

The density function is normalized automatically by $\ensuremath{\mathcal{M}}= \int f(x) dx$, so the PDF of the distribution is 

$$
\begin{aligned} \text{DensityFunctionPdf}(f, x) &= \frac{f(x)}{\ensuremath{\mathcal{M}}} \end{aligned} 
$$



The distribution can be specified either via the non-normalized density function $f(x)$ or via the non-normalized log-density function $\log(f(x))$: 

-   `density_function_dist`: Specified via the non-normalized density function $f(x)$ 
    -   `name`: custom string 
    -   `type`: `density_function_dist` 
    -   `function`: The density function $f$ 
-   `log_density_function_dist`: Specified via the log-density $\log(f(x))$ 
    -   `name`: custom string 
    -   `type`: `log_density_function_dist` 
    -   `function`: The logarithm $\log f$ of the non-normalized density function $f$ 

#### Array distribution {#sec:array-distribution label="sec:array-distribution"}

The Array distribution is a product of $n$ independent, one-dimensional distributions of one type, each over its own named observable:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{ArrayPdf}(\vec{x}) &= \prod_{i=1}^{n} m(x_i; p_{i,1},\cdots,p_{i,k})
\end{aligned}
$$

where $x_i$ is the observable named by `x` of the $i$-th entry of `components` and $p_{i,1},\cdots,p_{i,k}$ are the $k$ type-specific parameters of $m$ assigned in that entry or in `default_parameters`. The observables $x_i$ [must]{.smallcaps} be pairwise distinct. The Array distribution uses union composition (see [Composition of factors](#sec:composition){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:composition"}): each entry of `components` is one factor, identical to a distribution of the type `component_type` with the same `x` and the same parameters. Its density is that of the product distribution of these $n$ distributions, but it is declared flat and explicit such that engines [may]{.smallcaps} use a dedicated, vectorized implementation. Unlike an array of separate distributions, one draw from an Array distribution contains every component, and its evaluation returns one joint density.

An Array distribution is a struct defined by the following components:

- `name`: custom unique string
- `type`: `array_dist`
- `component_type`: `type` of the distribution $m$ shared by all components. It [must]{.smallcaps} be a univariate fundamental distribution whose variate component is called `x`; in this version of the standard these are `chebychev_dist`, `uniform_dist`, `exponential_dist`, `gaussian_dist` (or `normal_dist`), `generalized_normal_dist`, `lognormal_dist`, `landau_dist`, `poisson_dist`, `poisson_continuous_dist` and `polynomial_dist`. The type determines the parameters that [must]{.smallcaps} be specified for each component, either in its entry of `components` or in `default_parameters`
- `components`: array of structs, one per component of the product, each with:
  - `x`: name of the observable $x_i$. No two entries of `components` [may]{.smallcaps} name the same observable.
  - the parameters $p_{i,j}$ of the distribution $m$, each assigned to a number or name. If at least one parameter of at least one component is omitted here, the top-level component `default_parameters` becomes [required]{.smallcaps} and a default [must]{.smallcaps} be declared for that parameter.
- `default_parameters`: ([optional]{.smallcaps}) struct assigning parameters $p_{i,j}$ of the distribution $m$ to a number or name, applied to every component that does not specify them on its own

Names given in `components` or `default_parameters` [must]{.smallcaps} resolve to either a data set axis, parameter or function.

The parameters $p_{i,j}$ [must not]{.smallcaps} depend, directly or through functions, on any observable $x_k$ of the same Array distribution; they [may]{.smallcaps} share model parameters between components. Distinct observable names alone do not make the factors independent, and with a dependence between them the product would no longer be guaranteed to be normalized.

The typical usage, shown below, pairs the observables named by `x` with the axes of one unbinned data set holding auxiliary-measurement data for the constraint, with one axis per component and a single entry.


```json title="Example: Array distribution of type gaussian_dist"
{
    "distributions":[
        ...
            {
                "name":"gauss_constraints",
                "type":"array_dist",
                "component_type":"gaussian_dist",
                "default_parameters":{"sigma":"gaussian_constraints_sigma"},
                "components":[
                    {"x":"nom_np_1", "mean":"np_1", "sigma":"np_1_sigma"},
                    {"x":"nom_np_2", "mean":"np_2"},
                    {"x":"nom_np_3", "mean":"np_3", "sigma":31.622}
                ]
            }
        ...
    ],
    "data":[
        ...
        {
            "name":"nom_np",
            "type":"unbinned",
            "entries":[[0, 0, 0]],
            "axes":[
                {"name":"nom_np_1", "min":-5, "max":5},
                {"name":"nom_np_2", "min":-5, "max":5},
                {"name":"nom_np_3", "min":-5, "max":5}
            ]
        }
        ...
    ]
}
```


```json title="Example: Array distribution of type poisson_continuous_dist"
{
    "distributions":[
        ...
            {
                "name":"poisson_constraints",
                "type":"array_dist",
                "component_type":"poisson_continuous_dist",
                "default_parameters":{"mean":"lambda_poisson"},
                "components":[
                    {"x":"nom_np_1", "mean":"lambda_1"},
                    {"x":"nom_np_2"}
                ]
            }
        ...
    ],
    "functions":[
        {
            "name":"lambda_1",
            "type":"product",
            "factors":[20.0, "np_1"]
        },
        {
            "name":"lambda_poisson",
            "type":"product",
            "factors":[10.0, "np_poisson"]
        }
    ],
    "data":[
        ...
        {
            "name":"nom_np",
            "type":"unbinned",
            "entries":[[20, 10]],
            "axes":[
                {"name":"nom_np_1", "min":0, "max":100},
                {"name":"nom_np_2", "min":0, "max":100}
            ]
        }
        ...
    ]
}
```


#### Poisson point process {#dist:poisson-point-process label="dist:poisson-point-process"} 

A Poisson point process distribution is understood here as the distribution of the outcomes of a (typically inhomogeneous) Poisson point process. In particle physics, such a distribution is often called an extended distribution [@barlow-extended-ml].
In HS<sup>3</sup>, a Poisson point process distribution is specified using a global-rate parameter $\lambda$ and an underlying distribution $m$, either explicitly or implicitly (see below). 
Random values are drawn from the Poisson point process distribution by drawing a random number $n$ from a Poisson distribution of the global-rate $\lambda$, and then drawing $n$ random values from the underlying distribution $m$. The resulting random values are vectors $x$ of length $n$, so their length varies. 
The PDF the Poisson point process distribution at an outcome $x$ (a vector of length $n$) is 



$$
\begin{aligned} \text{PoissonPointProcessPdf}(\lambda, m, x) &= \text{PoissonPdf}(n, \lambda) \cdot \prod_{i=1}^n \text{PDF}(m, x_i). \end{aligned} 
$$



In particle physics, the function $\text{PoissonPointProcessPdf}(\lambda, m(\theta), x)$, for a fixed observation $x$ and varying parameters $\lambda$ and $\theta$, is often called an extended likelihood. 
A Poisson point process distribution can be specified in HS<sup>3</sup> in two ways: 

-   `rate_extended_dist`: The global-rate parameter $\lambda$     and underlying distribution $m$ are specified explicitly: 
    -   `name`: custom string 
    -   `type`: `rate_extended_dist` 
    -   `rate`: The global rate $\lambda$ 
    -   `dist`: The underlying distribution $m$ 

The name of the variable $x$ is taken from the underlying     distribution. The underlying distribution [must]{.smallcaps} not be     referred to from other components of the statistical model. 

-   `rate_density_dist`: Specified via a non-normalized rate-density function $f$. Both the global-rate parameter and the underlying distribution are implicit: $\lambda = \int f(y) d y$ and $m = \text{DensityFunctionPdf}(f)$. More formally, the distribution corresponds to the inhomogeneous Poisson point process that is defined by a non-normalized rate measure which has density $f$ in respect to the Lebesgue measure. 
    -   `name`: custom string 
    -   `type`: `rate_density_dist` 
    -   `x`: name of the variable $x$ 
    -   `density`: The rate-density function $f$ 

#### Bin-count distribution {#dist:bin-counts label="dist:bin-counts"} 

This is a binned version of the Poisson point process distribution 
([Poisson point process](#dist:poisson-point-process){reference-type="ref" reference="dist:poisson-point-process"}). It is the distribution of the bin counts that result from histogramming the outcomes of a Poisson point process distribution using a given binning scheme (see 
[Binned Data](#sec:binned-data){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:binned-data"}). 
Like the Poisson point process distribution, a Bin-counts distribution can either be specified via a global-rate parameter $\lambda$ and an underlying distribution $m$, or via a rate-density function $f$ (in which case $\lambda$ and $m$ are implicit). In addition, the binning scheme also has to be specified in either case, unless it can be inferred (see below). 
For $k$ bins, this type of distribution corresponds to a product of $k$ Poisson distributions with rates 



$$
\begin{aligned} \nu_i &= \lambda \cdot \int_{\text{bin}_i} \text{PDF}(m, y) dy \quad \text{equivalent to}\\ \nu_i &= \int_{\text{bin}_i} f(y) dy \end{aligned} 
$$



The PDF of the bin-counts distribution at an outcome $x$ (a vector of length $k$, same as the number of bins, ordered as defined in [Flattening of Binned Contents](#sec:axes-flattening){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:axes-flattening"}) is 



$$
\begin{aligned} \text{BinCountsPdf}(x) &= \prod_{i=1}^k \text{PoissonPdf}(x_i, \nu_i) \end{aligned} 
$$



-    `bincounts_extended_dist`: The global-rate parameter $\lambda$ and underlying distribution $m$ are specified explicitly: 
     -   `name`: custom string 
     -   `type`: `bincounts_extended_dist` 
     -   `rate`: The global rate $\lambda$ 
     -   `dist`: The underlying distribution $m$ 
     -   `axes`: array of binned axes defining the binning to be used, as defined in [Binned Axes](#sec:axes-binned){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:axes-binned"}. [optional]{.smallcaps} if `dist` is a binned distribution, in which case the same binning is used by default.  
	
The name of the variable $x$ is taken from the underlying distribution. The underlying distribution [must]{.smallcaps} not be referred to from other components of the statistical model. 

-   `bincounts_density_dist`: Specified via a non-normalized rate-density function $f$. Both the global-rate parameter and the underlying distribution are implicit: $\lambda = \int f(y) dy$ and $m = \text{DensityFunctionPdf}(f)$. 
    -   `name`: custom string 
    -   `type`: `bincounts_density_dist` 
    -   `x`: name of the variable $x$ 
    -   `density`: The rate-density function $f$ 
    -   `axes`: array of binned axes defining the binning to be used, as defined in [Binned Axes](#sec:axes-binned){reference-type="ref" reference="sec:axes-binned"}.
